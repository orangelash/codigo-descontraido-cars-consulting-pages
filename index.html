The advice service, in the open

Five questions that decide which classic you should buy

This is most of what the first conversation covers. If you only read one section of this site, read this one — it will save you more money than we will ever charge you.

  1. How will you actually use it?

    Sunday mornings, weekend touring, or something you genuinely drive during the week. The honest answer changes every other decision, and the honest answer is usually less often than people expect.

  2. What is the budget after the purchase?

    The purchase price is the deposit. A classic that has been sitting will ask for a first year of sorting, and that number belongs in the plan before you buy, not after.

  3. Can you get the parts?

    For some models a part is a phone call and a week. For others it is a forum, a fabricator and six months. Both are fine — but only one of them is a surprise.

  4. How much of it do you want to do yourself?

    A car that needs work is cheaper for a reason. If that reason is a hobby you want, it is the best value on the market. If it is not, it is the most expensive car you will ever buy.

  5. What happens when you sell it?

    Some classics hold their value, some are a hobby with a bill at the end. Neither is wrong. Knowing which one you are buying is the whole point.

What you can hold us to

Four things we will say that cost us money

“Don't buy it.”

Some cars are not worth the money, and some are not worth the money to you. You hear that while it is still free to walk away.

“Here is the rust.”

A condition report with no bad news in it is not a condition report. If we found it, it is in the file and it is in the photographs.

“It isn't ready.”

We do not sell a car we would not drive home ourselves. If it is not finished, it is not listed, however keen the buyer is.

“Someone else is better.”

On some cars a marque specialist beats a careful generalist. When that is true we say so, and we name one.

Before you ask

The questions we get every week

Do you only work on certain makes?

There is no fixed list. We take on what we can do properly, and we say so plainly when a car really wants a marque specialist instead.

Will you look at a car I'm about to buy?

Yes — that is the advice service, and it is the cheapest money you will spend on the car. It is also the only money you get back if the answer is no.

Can I see the work in progress?

Yes. And you get the photographs either way, so distance is not a reason to be left wondering.

Do you handle export?

Inspection, registration and transfer are handled in Portugal as standard. Export paperwork on request.

What does it cost?

Advice is a fixed fee agreed up front. Restoration is quoted in writing after the assessment. Nothing runs open-ended without a cap you agreed to.

Which language do we work in?

Portuguese or English, spoken and written — including the file itself.
